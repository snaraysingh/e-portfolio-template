Animation

CREATE • ANIMATE • EDUCATE

This project demonstrates how engaging educational content is created using animation, visual storytelling, and interactive design. A variety of techniques such as tweening, interpolation, scaling, and other visual effects were applied to bring this project's concept to life in a clear and engaging way. The project also demonstrates how effective animation and multimedia principles can enhance understanding and support knowledge retention. By combining creativity with design principles, the final product transforms information into a meaningful, immersive learning experience.

How The Eye Works Animated Video

An animation creation using Blender and Google Flow to create a short engaging lesson on how the human eye works. The animation takes learners on a visual journey zooming through the pupil and into an animated eye diagram that explains how we see. Transitions and visual effects were used to capture attention and support learner engagement and retention.

Project Reflection

Digital animation can be created using traditional animation software, AI animation generators, or a hybrid combination, as was done with this project. Both approaches have benefits and limitations, particularly in areas of creative control, skill requirements, and production time. The question, however, is: Are we gaining or losing by using AI in animation?

Using Blender, an open-source animation application, gave me an appreciation for the amount of work that goes into creating even a short animation. In The Journey From Light to Sight animation, I used keyframing, tweening, interpolation, scaling, rotation, and transitions to illustrate how the eye works. I also incorporated text and narration, paying particular attention to timing and the synchronization of visual and audio elements. Every movement was intentional and could be revised until the desired outcome was achieved. Although the traditional approach was time-consuming, it allowed me to develop technical skills while maintaining creative control.

With Google Flow, image assets and prompts were used to generate motion, camera movements, and visual effects. One major benefit of AI-generated animation was the speed at which content could be created. However, one of the biggest drawbacks was the lack of autonomy over the results. The output did not always align with my vision, and I had to continually revise my prompts to get closer to the intended outcome. Even with AI, patience and creativity were still needed.

Overall, this experience helped me understand how traditional and AI approaches can complement each other. As an instructional designer, I can see the benefit of using animation to simplify complex concepts and make learning content more engaging. I believe AI works best as a supportive and collaborative tool rather than as a replacement for the animator. As technology continues to speed up production and development, I am hopeful that creativity and technical skills will remain at the core of the animation process rather than becoming an afterthought to technology.
