INTERACTIVE LEARNING & EDUCATIONAL TECHNOLOGY

Screencasting

CAPTURE • DEMONSTRATE • EMPOWER

This project showcases using screencasting as a tool for teaching, guiding, and supporting learners. By combining screen capture, narration, zoom effects, and transitions, this project demonstrates how digital media can make learning more accessible, engaging, and effective while helping learners navigate tasks with confidence..

Step-by-Step Screencast Tutorial

Created with ScreenPal, this screencast provides viewers with a just-in-time tutorial on how to create and customize a column chart in Microsoft Excel. Through the use of zooming, highlighting, and transition effects, learners can easily follow each step of the process to gain a clear understanding.

Screencast Guide on using Google Vids

This screencast provides a walkthrough of Google Vids, an AI-assisted tool, for creating short educational videos. The demonstration shows the platform's main features using highlighting techniques to draw attention to key tools and functions used in creating learning content. The screencast was created with ScreenPal, ScreenText.io was used to provide the narration and the scenes were edited and exported with CapCut.

Project Reflection

What I found interesting about screencasting is that something as simple as recording your screen can become an effective instructional tool. However, there is a little more to the process than just recording your actions. It requires planning, organization, and an understanding of how to present information in a way that learners can easily follow.

For my first project, I created a step-by-step tutorial demonstrating how to create and customize a chart in Microsoft Excel. After experimenting with different screencasting applications, I settled on ScreenPal. I found the application quite intuitive and easy to navigate, especially for someone new to screencasting. During the process, I learned how to incorporate zooming and highlighting effects to draw the learner's attention to specific areas of the screen. I also realized the importance of pacing the narration and allowing enough time for learners to follow each step without feeling overwhelmed.

For my second project, I created a screencast guide demonstrating about Google Vids and how the AI is used to develop short educational videos. I used ScreenPal to record the demonstrations, CapCut to organize and edit the scenes, and SoundTools.io to generate the narration. Having worked with different multimedia applications throughout these projects, I am beginning to appreciate how various tools can complement each other. I also learned I can work with the tools I have access to and I do not necessarily need an expensive application with all the bells and whistles to produce effective instructional content.

Overall, I enjoyed creating both screencasts and can see myself incorporating this method into future instructional design projects. One of the benefits of screencasting is that learners can pause, revisit, and follow instructions at their own pace. As I continue on this journey, I would like to refine my screencasting and narration skills while exploring other ways to make tutorials more engaging and accessible. After all, the effectiveness of a tutorial is not solely determined by the sophistication of the technology, but by how well the learner understands the content.
