Project Reflection

Exploring both traditional and AI-assisted approaches to video production was quite a learning experience. Although both approaches shared some similarities, the amount of work involved was very different. With the traditional approach, I had to source the media, create a storyboard, write a script, organize the content, and bring everything together into a finished product. For the AI-assisted video, AI generated the media assets, storyboard, and script. My role was to import everything into the video editing application, arrange the content, and produce the video. While I was involved in editing and publishing both videos, AI significantly sped up the content development process.

Using AI was challenging at first because I spent a lot of time exploring different tools and trying to understand their capabilities. I experimented with Copilot, ChatGPT, Cana, Sora, Meta AI, and Google Veo/Flow, each having its own strengths and limitations. I felt immensely overwhelmed and unsure of where to start. Things turned around when Meta AI generated high-quality images and Google Flow transformed those images into animated video clips. It wasn't quite what I envisioned at first, but I realized I could build on it. AI gave me ideas and sped up content creation, but it also required patience, experimentation, and a lot of trial and error.

The experience was challenging but rewarding. I still prefer the traditional approach because it feels more familiar and gives me greater creative control, but AI introduced me to new ways of creating content. I improved my skills in storyboarding, video editing, transitions, pan-and-zoom effects, narration placement, and audio ducking. I also learned how to write better prompts, evaluate AI-generated content, and combine multiple AI tools.

I can see the value of incorporating AI into future instructional video production. AI can be a really useful tool, but creating meaningful and engaging learning content still requires human creativity, judgment, and direction.
