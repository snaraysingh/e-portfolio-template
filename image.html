DIGITAL DESIGN & CREATIVITY

Image Editing

CREATE • EDIT • INSPIRE

The image editing and graphic design projects on this page, highlight my passion for visual storytelling and creative expression. By combining design principles with digital editing techniques, I transformed concepts into compelling and memorable visuals. Each piece reflects the use of color, layout, imagery, and other design principles to create designs that are both aesthetically appealing and purposeful.

SeaShel Logo Design

Designed in Inkscape, this vector logo demonstrates my ability to create clean, scalable graphics using text and graphic elements. I used layers to organize and build each element of the logo. I created the pearl shell with the Bezier tool, and added the pearl using the shape tool. After refining the design, I grouped the elements and exported the finished logo. The logo can now be used in a variety of digital or print projects. This project strengthened my skills in vector-based design and highlights the importance of simplicity and balance in creating an effective logo design.

Project Reflection

Designing and developing images using traditional software and AI tools can be both entertaining and rewarding. Creating the SeaShel logo in Inkscape gave me an opportunity to explore vector graphics and experiment with fonts, color, shapes, and visual balance. It also helped me appreciate how simple design elements can work together to create a visually appealing and recognizable logo.

I prefer learning core editing and design skills through applications like Photoshop or GIMP, but it is evident that both traditional and AI approaches require user creativity. Whether using raster editing software or AI, the person guiding the process must have an idea or vision of the desired outcome. In both approaches, revisions can be made until a satisfactory result is achieved.

Although the outcome is different, both approaches use unique tools. Traditional editing software uses layers, brushes, and various editing features, while generative AI relies on prompts. One interesting question is, “Who really is the artist here?” With traditional software, the user has greater control over the design process and final output, whereas AI generates the artwork based on the details provided in the prompt.

In the book cover project, AI did not hinder the design process. I think I was a bit overzealous with my prompting because, while the result captured the essence of the Regency period, it contained more details than I intended. However, AI introduced some appealing design elements, such as the gold-trimmed border.

Overall, these projects helped me appreciate the value of both approaches. As an instructional designer, having these skills can help me create visually engaging educational materials that capture learners’ attention and support understanding and knowledge retention. While I welcome AI as a tool for brainstorming and design planning, I would much rather maintain creative control over the execution of the final design.
